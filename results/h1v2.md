# Track 2 — H1 v2: does steerability (eta_hat, Var_p(s)) predict per-question steering gain?

Date: 2026-10-15. Code: `truthfulqa_gemma2/h1v2.py`, `h1v2_analyze.py` (job 5625397). Data: `truthfulqa_gemma2/runs_h1v2/q*.json` (per-question summaries; raw score features not committed). Summary JSON: `results/h1v2.json`.

## Design (changes vs the pilot)

- Model Qwen3-1.7B; IDEEA's chat prompt (same as Track 1); intervention theta at layer 14, all positions from the last prompt token on (IDEEA's CAA protocol); sampling at T = 1, 50 tokens.
- Trait score s = truth AND info with the **Qwen3-1.7B proxy judge** (threshold calibrated on dev reference answers; see Track 1 for validation); embedding-judge scores stored as a secondary check.
- 60 test-split questions drawn at random (seed 0) from the 249/395 whose 16 screening samples had 0 < p < 1 (screening samples not reused).
- Per question: 128 fresh base samples for fitting (two halves of 64 → split-half reliability), 128 fresh base samples for the baseline score, and 128 evaluation samples per (direction, budget).
- Directions: **pq** = per-question natural gradient restricted to a k = 32 sketch (top-32 eigenvectors of the global within-question Fisher G from the train split); **rand** = random; **caa** = IDEEA's CAA direction at L14; **ngG** = Track 1's global natural-gradient direction.
- **Equal realized KL:** alpha set from the sample Fisher, then re-calibrated with 32 samples (KL ∝ alpha²), then KL re-measured on the 128 evaluation samples. Budgets: sequence KL 0.05 and 0.2.
- **Better KL estimate:** Rao-Blackwellised sequence KL, sum_t KL(p_theta(.|y<t) || p_0(.|y<t)) for y ~ p_theta. Its standard error is 3–13x smaller (medians) than the plain log-ratio estimator's (e.g. ngG@0.2: 0.009 vs 0.056).
- eta_hat = cross-fitted (2-fold) held-out R² of s on the sketch features, floored at 0; eta = Var_p(s) x eta_hat.

## Results (60 questions; SE over questions)

Realized KL matched well: medians 0.048–0.051 (target 0.05) and 0.19–0.21 (target 0.2). One CAA outlier inflates CAA's mean KL at 0.05 (0.172); its median is 0.049. For directions not fitted on the same samples (rand, caa, ngG), the quadratic Fisher prediction 1/2 alpha² vᵀĜv matches the realized RB-KL (median ratio 0.94–0.98). For pq it under-predicts (0.55–0.62), because pq is fitted on those same samples.

Base p (Qwen judge) on these questions averages 0.31; Var_p(s) averages 0.13.

**Bound eta ≤ Var_p(s).** Its operational, first-order form is gain ≤ sqrt(2·KL·Var_p(s)) for any direction (to second order in KL; the exact Cauchy–Schwarz form uses chi²). It is not contradicted: in no (question, direction, budget) cell does the gain exceed the bound by more than 2 SE, and the raw share above the bound is 0–8%. But this test has low power. Gains are only 24–39% of the bound, and the within-question SE (0.043) is a large fraction of the bound (≈0.10 at KL 0.05). The direct check from the follow-up (below) is stronger: the split-sample estimate satisfies eta ≤ Var_p(s) for 100% of questions.

**Reliability of eta_hat (k = 32, pre-registered estimator = floored cross-fitted R²).** The split-half Pearson correlation across two independent sets of 64 samples is 0.65 (Spearman 0.60, n = 47 usable). Without the floor at 0 it is 0.34. The other sketch sizes give 0.63–0.86 (k = 8 to 256). This is better than the pilot (0.49 at n = 32, full dimension) but still below the pre-set 0.7. It is also carried by a few questions: with all 128 samples, eta_hat = 0 for 48 of 60 questions, eta_hat > 0 for 9, and it is undefined for 3. Among the 47 usable pairs, 36 are 0/0; dropping the top pair gives r = 0.49; 13 questions drop out as NaN (a half with Var = 0). Reviewer finding: the unfloored cross-fitted R² averages −0.91 (32 sketch features, 32–64 training samples, weak ridge), so this estimator badly overfits and is biased toward 0. On questions with eta_hat = 0, the ngG and pq directions still reach efficiency 0.12 and 0.07. The floored R² is therefore NOT a usable estimate of eta; see the follow-up.

## Follow-up (post hoc, CPU, from the saved score features; `truthfulqa_gemma2/h1v2_eta2.py` → `results/h1v2_eta2.json`)

**(a) Per-question first-order prediction along a fixed direction.** For a direction v that was not fitted on these samples, theory predicts dT/ds = Cov(s, u·v) / sd(u·v). We estimate it from the 128 fit samples and compare it with the realized efficiency on independent evaluation samples:

| direction | Spearman (KL 0.05 / 0.2) | Pearson (0.2) | slope (0.2) | mean predicted vs realized | partial ρ given Var (0.2) [95% CI] |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ngG | 0.60 [0.40, 0.75] / **0.73 [0.56, 0.85]** | 0.78 | 0.90 | 0.135 vs 0.132 | **0.70 [0.49, 0.83]** |
| caa | 0.27 / 0.32 | 0.47 | 0.77 | 0.003 vs −0.021 | 0.28 |
| rand | 0.15 / 0.48 | 0.56 | 0.60 | 0.001 vs −0.009 | 0.48 |
| pq (fitted on the same samples) | 0.35 / 0.54 | 0.56 | 0.70 | 0.26 vs 0.09 (in-sample optimism) | – |

For a fixed direction, the score-function first-order formula predicts which questions it will move and by how much. It is calibrated in the mean for ngG, and it works far better than Var alone (ρ 0.33) or the Euclidean features (|ρ| ≤ 0.33).

**(b) A split-sample estimator of eta.** eta_split = g_1ᵀ M g_2, where g_1 and g_2 come from disjoint halves of the fit samples and M is the inverse sketch metric; it is averaged over 5 random splits. Unlike the floored R², it is unbiased for the quadratic form (up to the metric estimate):
- Reliability across the two independent 64-sample halves: k = 32: Pearson 0.85, Spearman 0.71 (meets the 0.7 target); k = 8: 0.72 / 0.48; k = 16: 0.74 / 0.59. Negative estimates: 12–27%.
- The bound eta ≤ Var_p(s) holds for 100% of questions (k = 8, 16, 32). Median eta_hat = eta / Var is 0.05–0.12.
- Prediction of pq efficiency at KL 0.2 beyond Var (partial Spearman): k = 8: 0.50 [0.18, 0.72]; k = 32: 0.19 [−0.08, 0.49]. For ngG: k = 8: 0.23 [−0.09, 0.50]. k = 8 was chosen after looking (post hoc).
- With the global metric instead of the per-question one, the estimate is reliable (r = 0.90), but it is not bounded by Var (mean 0.91 vs Var 0.13): it is not the paper's eta.

## Reading

Solid:
- At equal realized KL, both natural-gradient directions raise the judged score over random and CAA directions (ngG − random +0.087 ± 0.012 at KL 0.2; 85% of questions improve). The global direction beats the per-question sketch direction.
- **The first-order score-function prediction works per question for a fixed direction** (follow-up a): ρ = 0.73 between predicted and realized efficiency of ngG, slope 0.90, mean calibrated, partial ρ given Var 0.70. This is the most direct support for the framework so far: the readout-consistent gradient, estimated from base samples alone, tells you how far a given intervention will move each input.
- The KL machinery is sound: realized RB-KL is matched across directions, and the quadratic Fisher prediction is accurate (ratio 0.94–0.98) for directions not fitted on the same samples.
- The bound eta ≤ Var_p(s) is consistent with the data: the split-sample estimate is ≤ Var for every question, and no steering gain exceeds the first-order bound beyond noise. Both tests have modest power.

Not solid:
- **The pre-registered eta_hat (floored cross-fitted R²) is unusable**: it is biased toward 0 by overfitting. Its correlations with gain (ρ 0.41 at pq @ 0.2) should not be read as a test of H1.
- "eta predicts beyond Var" is mixed. With the split-sample estimator: yes at k = 8 for pq (partial 0.50, CI excludes 0), but k was chosen post hoc; no at the pre-chosen k = 32 (0.19, CI includes 0); not shown for ngG. A pre-registered replication on fresh questions is needed.
- The Euclidean features are weak but not useless (first-token entropy ρ = 0.33 for ngG @ 0.2, comparable to Var).
- At KL 0.05 the per-question gains are not reliable (split-half SB ≈ 0), so only the KL 0.2 targets are informative.
- Judge circularity: ngG was fitted with the same Qwen proxy used for scoring. With the embedding judge the gains are 0.35–0.51 of the Qwen-judge values (pq @ 0.05 +0.011 ± 0.007, not significant).
- Selection: the questions have 0 < p < 1 under the same proxy. Gains near p ≈ 0 are floor-limited. The CAA @ 0.05 cell contains one KL outlier (7.3).
