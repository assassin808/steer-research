- IDEA · (user questions) Foundations to settle: (1) mathematical definition of a trait; (2) is the trait flow learnable by RL; (3) if all valid sentences lie on one manifold, what is a trait on it. Paper C: why does pure activation-space geometry fail to predict steerability -> we have not found the true reach.
  Proposed answers (to review): trait = scalar field T(h) = expected trait score of the model's continuation from state h (behavioral, judge/probe-defined), i.e. a coordinate function on the natural-state manifold, not a separate manifold; its level sets foliate the manifold. Canonical trait flow = natural-gradient ascent of T under the pullback Fisher metric (max trait gain per unit output change) -> removes arbitrariness of "the" direction. Output-space ideal = exponential tilt p_t ∝ p_0 exp(t r_trait) (optimum of KL-regularized RL), an e-geodesic; so RL with reward = trait score − λ·KL can learn an activation-space intervention realizing it, and t ↔ 1/λ gives a principled intensity coordinate (replaces failed adverb prompts). Paper C: steerability is a property of the map h -> output (Jacobian / pullback metric) and of the trait functional, not of activation geometry alone; Euclidean reach of the prompt cloud is the wrong object. Behavioral reach = largest step at which steering still tracks the tilt path (KL to nearest tilted distribution small). Hypothesis: pullback/tilt features predict steerability, Euclidean features do not. Evidence that Euclidean features fail: still to collect (Tan et al. 2024 report unreliable steerability; verify).
- IDEA · (discussion) (1) Gradient of T (perpendicular to level sets) maximizes trait gain per unit step only w.r.t. the chosen metric; with the full next-token Fisher metric it minimizes TOTAL output change for a given gain (dual problems), but not change in specific other traits (needs constraint: project out other traits' gradients = least-interference), not natural-ness of later tokens (next-token KL is one-step), and only infinitesimally (finite steps: greedy natural-gradient flow != minimal-cost geodesic path). (2) KL alone does not describe the manifold: it gives a (semi-definite) local metric, blind to support (which states the model actually reaches), degenerate on ker G, one-step myopic. Need support/density + behavioral metric (+ sequence-level KL where affordable). (3) Paper C reach: behavioral validity radius = scale where the quadratic law breaks; estimate as ratio of 2nd to 3rd directional derivative of D(alpha) (Taylor radius) from a JVP + 2-3 forward passes per (input, direction); combine with support-exit distance; separately the trait saturation edge (dT/dalpha -> 0). Predict steerability from (eta, r).
